# Decraft

A professional work archive where artisans build a public portfolio from the projects they document.

## The Unique Kernel
Projects—not general social posts—are the building blocks of an artisan's professional reputation. Each project can hold its description, media, and progress over time, then become reusable portfolio evidence.

## Who It's For
Independent artisans and small workshops who currently keep examples of their work across phone galleries, WhatsApp conversations, and social media. Customers looking for a relevant professional are the audience for the resulting public portfolio.

## The Core Loop
An artisan creates multiple projects from one account. Each project has its own space for its description, media, and progress log. The artisan reviews what will be public and chooses when to share it. Signed-in users browse the in-app maker directory, open projects to understand the work, and can make an inquiry. The artisan returns to add progress or another project, making the portfolio more useful over time.

## Inspiration & Identity
The learner describes the product as a “GitHub for craftsmen.” Take inspiration from GitHub's project-centered structure: one maker owns multiple distinct workspaces, and each project has its own overview and activity history. Do not copy GitHub's code-specific interface or terminology. The PRD's positioning is “Your craft. Your work. Your reputation.” The landing page should tell the story from scattered work examples, to organized project spaces, to customers discovering and contacting a professional. The whole app should work responsively on mobile and larger screens.

## Why This Matters to the Learner
The learner wants craftsmen to have a dedicated professional home for their work and portfolio. More personal motivation has not been established.

## What "Working" Looks Like
A short demo begins with one landing page that explains the product, includes its how-it-works story, and previews a few projects. Find work, maker profiles, and project details are inside the authenticated app. A signed-in maker sees a collection of projects, creates more than one, and opens each into its own overview, progress log, and media space. The maker can then review and choose what to share. A signed-in customer finds the maker through discovery, opens a project, and starts an inquiry. The project is the evidence; it is not presented as independently verified proof of quality. The landing page and signed-in app adapt to phone and larger screen sizes.

## The POC Boundary
The learner wants the full P0 set in the existing PRD treated as the MVP, delivered in stages. The first demonstrable slice should establish the unique kernel: an artisan manages multiple distinct project spaces, records progress, explicitly reviews what to share, and a customer can view a published project as portfolio evidence. Keep the remaining P0 capabilities in the staged MVP plan rather than silently removing them. Photo/video selection, durable storage, and AI-assisted descriptions must be clearly distinguished from the current browser preview until their services are connected.

## Later
P1 storage tiers, business analytics, and paid features; P2 reviews and ratings, workshop team management, payments and contracts, and advanced verification, as classified in the existing PRD.

## Explicitly Cut
- A general-purpose social feed, likes, and popularity rankings: they distract from work as the primary content.
- Full job bidding, escrow, and dispute resolution: explicitly outside the MVP described in the PRD.
- Ratings or craftsmanship verification: project records are user-submitted evidence, not independent certification.
